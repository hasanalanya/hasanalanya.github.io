01 Istanbul, Türkiye · 2014–2019

Learning to read biology as data

I trained in Genetics and Bioengineering at Istanbul Bilgi University on a national ÖSYM scholarship. Research at Koç University's computational biology lab, predicting protein–protein interactions with the PRISM algorithm and building a dataset of protein interfaces, showed me that biology could be read computationally.

My bachelor's thesis put that to work: machine learning on microarray data to sort gastric cancer into molecular subtypes and find biomarkers for each.

  • BSc Genetics & Bioengineering, Istanbul Bilgi University
  • Two-time Koç University Summer Research Program scholar
  • Thesis on gastric-cancer subtyping, awarded the highest grade

02 Stockholm, Sweden · 2019–2021

From single molecules to whole systems

For my master's I moved to Stockholm, to a joint programme of Karolinska Institutet, KTH and Stockholm University. In Prof. Adil Mardinoglu's systems medicine group at SciLifeLab I analysed proteomics, transcriptomics, metabolomics and metagenomics data, and learned to choose the right statistics for each.

My thesis built gene co-expression networks of muscle tissue to uncover genes and pathways behind sarcopenia, the age-related loss of muscle. It was my first step into the biology of ageing.

  • MSc Molecular Techniques in Life Science
  • Multi-omics statistics at SciLifeLab
  • Co-author of a drug-repositioning study for sarcopenia (under review)

03 New Haven, United States · 2022–2025

Genomics at the bedside

At Yale School of Medicine's Department of Neurosurgery I joined the Erson Lab, co-supervised by Prof. Murat Günel. For nearly four years I integrated genomic, transcriptomic and epigenetic data from primary brain tumours, working with clinical teams to understand rare subtypes, how tumours evolve and how they interact with the immune system.

The work contributed to six peer-reviewed papers, including a meningioma subgroup defined by FOS/FOSB gene fusions in Nature Communications. I presented our pituitary-adenoma genomics at the Society for Neuro-Oncology meeting in Houston.

  • 6 peer-reviewed papers, 2 as first author
  • Nature Communications, 2025
  • Mentored three summer research students

04 Birmingham, United Kingdom · 2025–now

Targeting ageing itself

Today I'm a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Doctoral Fellow at the University of Birmingham, in Prof. João Pedro de Magalhães's Genomics of Ageing and Rejuvenation Lab. I build interpretable models (network methods, graph neural networks and causal ML) that integrate multi-omics data to find what drives ageing.

Then I map drug–gene–pathway interactions onto those signatures to predict which existing drugs could slow ageing and frailty. I continue to collaborate with Yale as a Research Affiliate.

  • Marie Skłodowska-Curie Doctoral Fellowship, Horizon Europe
  • Interpretable graph neural networks and causal ML
  • Research Affiliate, Yale School of Medicine

The approach

No approved drug targets ageing itself. Some existing drugs may already slow it. Interpretable AI can tell us which ones to test first, and why.

  1. 01

    Signatures

    Integrate genomic, transcriptomic, epigenetic and proteomic data to find the molecular signatures that drive ageing.

  2. 02

    Networks

    Map drug–gene–pathway interactions onto those signatures, building a network of where each compound acts.

  3. 03

    Candidates

    Rank existing drugs with graph neural networks and causal ML, with the mechanism attached to every prediction.
