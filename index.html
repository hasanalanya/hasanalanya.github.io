01 / 042014 — 2019

Istanbul Türkiye

Learning to read biology as data

I trained in Genetics and Bioengineering at Istanbul Bilgi University on a national ÖSYM scholarship. At Koç University's computational biology lab I predicted protein–protein interactions with the PRISM algorithm and built a dataset of protein interfaces.

My bachelor's thesis used machine learning on microarray data to sort gastric cancer into molecular subtypes and find biomarkers for each.

  • BSc · Genetics & Bioengineering
  • Two-time Koç summer research scholar
  • Thesis awarded the highest grade

02 / 042019 — 2021

Stockholm Sweden

From single molecules to whole systems

For my master's I moved to Stockholm, to a joint programme of Karolinska Institutet, KTH and Stockholm University. In Prof. Adil Mardinoglu's systems medicine group at SciLifeLab I analysed proteomics, transcriptomics, metabolomics and metagenomics data.

My thesis built gene co-expression networks of muscle tissue to uncover the genes and pathways behind sarcopenia, the age-related loss of muscle. It was my first step into the biology of ageing.

  • MSc · Molecular Techniques in Life Science
  • Multi-omics statistics at SciLifeLab
  • Sarcopenia drug-repositioning study (under review)

03 / 042022 — 2025

New Haven United States

Genomics at the bedside

At Yale School of Medicine's Department of Neurosurgery I joined the Erson Lab, co-supervised by Prof. Murat Günel. For nearly four years I integrated genomic, transcriptomic and epigenetic data from primary brain tumours, working with clinical teams on rare subtypes and tumour evolution.

The work contributed to six peer-reviewed papers, including a meningioma subgroup defined by FOS/FOSB gene fusions in Nature Communications. I presented our pituitary-adenoma genomics at the Society for Neuro-Oncology meeting in Houston.

  • Postgraduate Research Associate · Yale
  • 6 papers, 2 as first author
  • Mentored three research students

04 / 042025 — now

Birmingham United Kingdom

Targeting ageing itself

Today I'm a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Doctoral Fellow at the University of Birmingham, in Prof. João Pedro de Magalhães's Genomics of Ageing and Rejuvenation Lab. I build interpretable models (network methods, graph neural networks and causal ML) that integrate multi-omics data to find what drives ageing.

Then I map drug–gene–pathway interactions onto those signatures to predict which existing drugs could slow ageing and frailty. I still collaborate with Yale as a Research Affiliate.

  • PhD · MSCA Doctoral Fellow
  • Graph neural networks & causal ML
  • Research Affiliate, Yale

Fellowship

Funded by the EU's flagship programme for doctoral research.

My PhD is supported by a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Doctoral Fellowship under Horizon Europe. It funds three years of research at the University of Birmingham's Institute of Inflammation and Ageing.

No approved drug targets ageing itself. Some existing drugs may already slow it. Interpretable AI can tell us which ones to test first, and why.

  1. 01

    Signatures

    Integrate genomic, transcriptomic, epigenetic and proteomic data to find the molecular signatures that drive ageing.

  2. 02

    Networks

    Map drug–gene–pathway interactions onto those signatures to see where each compound acts.

  3. 03

    Candidates

    Rank existing drugs with graph neural networks and causal ML, with the mechanism attached to every prediction.
