About

I'm a computational biologist and Marie Skłodowska-Curie Doctoral Fellow at the University of Birmingham. I combine machine learning, network biology and multi-omics data to ask which molecular programmes drive ageing and disease, and which existing drugs might reverse them.

Before my PhD, I spent nearly four years in the Erson Lab at Yale School of Medicine's Department of Neurosurgery, building genomic analyses of primary brain tumours with a focus on rare subtypes, tumour evolution and tumour–immune interactions. That work led to papers in Nature Communications, Frontiers in Oncology, Neuro-Oncology Advances and Pituitary, and I continue to collaborate with the lab as a Research Affiliate.

My path runs from structural bioinformatics at Koç University, through systems medicine at SciLifeLab in Stockholm, to clinical cancer genomics in the US and now ageing research in the UK. Throughout, I aim for reproducible pipelines and models whose predictions a biologist can question and test at the bench.
